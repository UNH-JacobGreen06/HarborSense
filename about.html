Ocean Pollution Overview

The Problem

Many marinas lack the tools to monitor pollution effectively. As a result, harmful substances such as oil, plastics, and chemicals accumulate over time, damaging ecosystems and threatening marine life.

80% of ocean pollution comes from land-based sources, often carried into waterways during storms.

Why This Matters

Because most pollution originates on land, it is often carried into coastal waters through runoff, especially during storms. This type of pollution, known as nonpoint source pollution, does not come from a single identifiable source, making it difficult to track and control.

Without real-time monitoring, pollution can build up unnoticed, leading to long-term environmental damage and increased risks to both ecosystems and human health.

How HarborSense Works

Inspired by modern environmental monitoring technologies, HarborSense transforms raw environmental data into actionable insights that communities can use to make informed decisions.

Step Description
Data Collection Sensors collect real-time data such as pH, turbidity, and temperature
Data Transmission Collected data is sent to a central monitoring system
Analysis Patterns and environmental changes are analyzed
Alert System Alerts are triggered when pollution levels rise
Community Action Communities take proactive action before environmental damage occurs

Global plastic production has increased dramatically since 1950, growing more than 200 times. As production increases, so does the risk of pollution entering marine ecosystems.

Storms and Their Impact

Severe storms and heavy rainfall significantly increase ocean pollution, especially in coastal communities. During storms, large amounts of runoff from roads, lawns, and urban areas are washed into nearby waterways. This runoff often carries oil, chemicals, plastics, and other harmful pollutants directly into marinas and oceans. Storm events are one of the leading causes of sudden spikes in water pollution levels.

Because most ocean pollution comes from land-based sources, storm events can rapidly worsen water quality in a very short amount of time. Without real-time monitoring, these changes often go undetected until damage has already occurred.

Real-World Impact

Pollution can lead to harmful algal blooms, which reduce oxygen levels in the water and create “dead zones” where marine life cannot survive. These events are becoming more common and are often difficult to predict without continuous monitoring.

HarborSense provides early warnings that allow communities to take action before these environmental issues become severe.
