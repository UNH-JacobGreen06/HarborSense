Final Reflection

Strengths you discovered about yourselves during the community-engagement learning experience.

During this experience, I discovered that I am strong in problem-solving and adapting to new challenges. I was able to take an idea and develop it into a more structured and realistic solution. I also found that I work well when collaborating with others and can contribute effectively to group discussions and decision-making. One specific strength that stood out was our ability to explain technical concepts — like how water sensors work — in plain language that a general audience could actually understand and care about.

Challenges you faced in dealing with other people during the course of your community-engagement project.

One of the main challenges was coordinating ideas and ensuring everyone was on the same page. At times, different perspectives made it difficult to decide on the best approach. However, this experience helped me understand the importance of communication, compromise, and listening to others in order to move forward as a team. Reaching out to external organizations like the Marine Science Center at Canal Dock also required a level of professionalism and persistence that pushed us outside our comfort zones.

Qualities about yourselves that you learned you'd like to work on more in the future.

I would like to improve my time management and organization skills, especially when working on larger projects. Additionally, I want to become more confident in presenting ideas and taking initiative in group settings. I also realized I tend to wait until something feels fully polished before sharing it, when getting early feedback would have saved time and led to a better result. These are important skills that will help me both academically and professionally.

Memorable things you learned about the real-world partners and the organizations you interacted with (i.e. what it does, its purpose, its mission, its history, its programs…) Be specific, give examples.

One memorable takeaway came from reaching out to the Marine Science Center at Canal Dock at the University of New Haven, which conducts real environmental monitoring research on Long Island Sound. Learning that even a well-resourced research facility struggles with making their data accessible to the general public reinforced why HarborSense addresses a genuine need. We also learned from Oceana that effective environmental organizations focus on specific, winnable goals rather than trying to solve everything at once — a strategy that shaped how we think about rolling out HarborSense in phases.

One thing you learned about the real-world people served by their organizations that was surprising to you or made you think of issues you may have not paid attention to before.

One thing that surprised me is how many communities are directly affected by environmental issues like water pollution but may not have access to the tools or resources to monitor or address them. Before this project, I thought of water quality monitoring mainly as a scientific issue. Working on HarborSense made me realize it is also a matter of access — the people who need the information most are often the last to receive it. Even small improvements in accessibility and awareness can have a significant real-world impact.

One thing you learned that you wish more people knew about

One important thing I learned is that environmental problems are often not due to a lack of solutions, but rather a lack of accessibility, awareness, and implementation. Many effective ideas already exist, but making them affordable and scalable is the real challenge. HarborSense is our attempt to close that gap, and I think more people should understand how important it is to bridge it.
