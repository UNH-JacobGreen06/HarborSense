Final Reflection

Strengths you discovered about yourselves during the community-engagement learning experience.

During this experience, I discovered that I am strong in problem-solving and adapting to new challenges. I was able to take an idea and develop it into a more structured and realistic solution. I also found that I work well when collaborating with others and can contribute effectively to group discussions and decision-making.

Challenges you faced in dealing with other people during the course of your community-engagement project.

One of the main challenges was coordinating ideas and ensuring everyone was on the same page. At times, different perspectives made it difficult to decide on the best approach. However, this experience helped me understand the importance of communication, compromise, and listening to others in order to move forward as a team.

Qualities about yourselves that you learned you’d like to work on more in the future.

I would like to improve my time management and organization skills, especially when working on larger projects. Additionally, I want to become more confident in presenting ideas and taking initiative in group settings. These are important skills that will help me both academically and professionally.

Memorable things you learned about the real-world partners and the organizations you interacted with (i.e. what it does, its purpose, its mission, its history, its programs…) Be specific, give examples.

One memorable takeaway is how organizations focused on environmental protection prioritize both sustainability and practicality. For example, many environmental agencies aim to balance cost, efficiency, and long-term impact when implementing solutions. I learned that even well-designed technologies must meet real-world constraints such as funding, maintenance, and usability in order to be successful.

One thing you learned about the real-world people served by their organizations that was surprising to you or made you think of issues you may have not paid attention to before.

One thing that surprised me is how many communities are directly affected by environmental issues like water pollution but may not have access to tools or resources to monitor or address them. This made me realize that even small improvements in accessibility and awareness can have a significant impact.

One thing you learned that you wish more people knew about

One important thing I learned is that environmental problems are often not due to a lack of solutions, but rather a lack of accessibility, awareness, and implementation. Many effective ideas already exist, but making them affordable and scalable is the real challenge. I think more people should understand how important it is to bridge that gap.
